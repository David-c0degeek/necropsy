## Captain Hindsight calibration

> Append or update one row for each `PM-*` risk relevant to the completed review
> target. Do not edit the prediction register to make the prediction look better
> in hindsight.
>
> Outcome is one of:
> - `Prevented Prediction` — the risk was credible and the guardrail prevented it.
> - `Confirmed Prediction` — the predicted failure occurred or was observed during verification.
> - `False Positive / Noise` — later evidence shows the prediction was not materially credible.
> - `Inconclusive` — evidence or observation horizon is insufficient.
>
> A risk that simply has not happened *yet* is not automatically a false positive.
> If its predicted horizon has not elapsed, use `Inconclusive`.
>
> Observed impact: `None`, `Low`, `Medium`, `High`, `Critical`, or `Unknown`.
> Actual detection: `Prevented`, `Before merge`, `Before production`, `In production`, `Silent`, `Not observed`, or `Unknown`.
> Mitigation effectiveness: `Effective`, `Partial`, `Ineffective`, `Not exercised`, or `N/A`.
> Severity calibration: `Underestimated`, `Accurate`, `Overestimated`, or `Not observable`.
>
> The table begins empty. Add a row only when Captain Hindsight has evidence to
> calibrate that prediction; use `Inconclusive` when evidence is insufficient.

## Calibration questions

Captain Hindsight should be able to answer, from this file and the implementation evidence:

- Did we predict the right failure class?
- Did we predict its severity accurately?
- Did we predict where it would be detected accurately?
- Did the chosen guardrail actually work?
- What escaped prediction entirely?

The goal is not a perfect pessimism score. It is a progressively better ability
to notice the failures that matter before they ship.
